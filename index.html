Methodology

Bilateral merchandise trade at HS6 product level, estimated on BACI 2012–2024 and projected to 2070. Three scenario channels sit on one pipeline: the EXIOMOD GDP overlay, the TIAM CO₂ pathway, and EXIOMOD sectoral growth. All three are imposed inputs; none is fitted to trade data.

Step 1 — chapter totals (PPML gravity)

One Poisson regression per HS2 chapter sets each country pair’s trade within that chapter. Economic size enters as a fixed offset on log GDP for both partners with the elasticity held at 1, so trade grows with the economy rather than faster. Estimated regressors: log distance, a damped time trend (0.25 beyond 2024), and a chapter-specific exporter CO₂ elasticity.

Step 2 — sectoral tilt (imposed)

Chapter totals are reweighted by EXIOMOD sectoral output growth, downscaled from the 17 EXIOMOD regions to individual countries using World Bank sector value-added shares combined with each country’s BACI sector composition, then renormalised so every country’s total is unchanged. The channel therefore moves composition and never levels. It applies at chapter level only: the weight is constant within a chapter, so it would divide out of the Step 3 share renormalisation.

Step 3 — product shares within each chapter

A gradient-boosting model predicts the year-on-year change in each HS6 line’s logit share of its parent chapter. Features: the pair’s recent share level, trend and volatility, log distance, a product identifier, the three product exposure scores (fossil, environmental good, low-carbon technology), and each of those interacted with exporter CO₂. Predicted shares are rescaled exactly to the Step 2 chapter total, so adding-up is exact by construction rather than by approximation.

λ(region, year) is the ratio of a scenario’s regional GDP to the same region’s GDP under the reference scenario. It scales GDP only; population stays on the SSP2 path. λ = 1 for all years through 2024, so the estimation window is untouched. CO₂ coefficients are fitted on a fixed reference pathway, because the TIAM scenarios already diverge inside the estimation window; CO₂ then varies only in projection.

What is estimated and what is assumed

Estimated: gravity structure, chapter CO₂ elasticities, HS6 share allocation. Imposed: the unit income elasticity, the TIAM pathway, the EXIOMOD λ overlay and the sectoral tilt. The sectoral channel cannot be estimated: the EXIOMOD scenarios are identical before 2025, so within-cell scenario variance over the estimation window is 0.0018 per cent of total, and a coefficient fitted there would be identified off the downscaling key rather than off scenario variation. Not supported by the data: a green reallocation mechanism — the cross-chapter test of whether fossil-exposed chapters contract faster as exporters decarbonise returns the wrong sign and is not statistically significant, and the HS6 CO₂ interactions carry negligible feature importance. Fuels results should be read as a scenario assumption, not as a model finding.

Known artefact: the CO₂ variable is a standardised level rather than a deviation from 2024, so the channel re-levels at the join between fitted history and projection. Direction after roughly 2040 is credible; timing before then is not.

On interpretation: the sectoral channel carries EXIOMOD’s own sectoral reallocation. Where these results agree with EXIOMOD on composition, that is transmission rather than independent corroboration — this model adds bilateral structure and HS6 resolution to EXIOMOD’s answer.
